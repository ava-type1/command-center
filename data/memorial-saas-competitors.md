# Memorial Pages SaaS — Competitive Research

**Date:** February 9, 2026

---

## The Landscape

The memorial/funeral tech space is bigger than expected but fragmented. There are several categories of players, and **nobody is doing exactly what Kam is proposing** (AI-powered living memorials with historical newspaper research, sold B2B to funeral homes).

---

## Category 1: Full Funeral Home Software Platforms (B2B)

These are comprehensive case management systems that include memorial pages as ONE feature among many.

### Tribute Technology (tributetech.com)
- **What:** Full-suite funeral home platform — websites, case management, payments, tribute videos
- **Memorial pages:** Yes, but bundled with their full platform
- **Pricing:** Enterprise — custom quotes, likely $300-500+/month
- **AI:** Recently launched "Tribute Spotlights" broadcast memorial platform on local TV stations
- **Size:** Major player, serving thousands of funeral homes
- **Weakness:** Massive platform — memorial pages are an afterthought, not the focus. Expensive. Built for big operations.

### Gather (gather.app)
- **What:** Case management platform with "Remember Pages" (memorial pages)
- **Memorial pages:** Included with case management — families can upload photos, add memories
- **Pricing:** Custom quotes only — likely $200-400/month+
- **AI:** Has an AI Obituary Builder (generates obituary text from basic info)
- **Weakness:** Memorial pages are a side feature, not the core product. No newspaper research. No QR codes. Focused on case management.

### funeralOne (funeralone.com)
- **What:** Website design, tribute video software, consulting
- **Memorial pages:** Part of their website packages
- **Pricing:** Custom
- **Weakness:** Old-school approach. Website design focus, not memorial innovation.

### Tukios (tukios.com)
- **What:** Website software + tribute video creation
- **Pricing:** Custom
- **Weakness:** Primarily focused on tribute videos and funeral home websites. Not a standalone memorial product.

### 1Director / Passare
- **What:** Case management software
- **Memorial pages:** Can publish to memorial websites
- **Weakness:** Management tools, not memorial-focused

**Key insight:** These platforms bundle memorial pages into expensive, comprehensive software suites. A funeral home that just wants to offer memorial pages has to buy the whole platform. **There's a gap for a focused, affordable memorial-only product.**

## Category 2: Direct-to-Consumer Memorial Websites

These go straight to families, cutting out funeral homes.

### EverLoved (everloved.com)
- **What:** Free memorial pages + AI obituary writing + funeral planning
- **Traffic:** Significant (~competitor to Echovita at 1.9M monthly visits)
- **Pricing:** Free basic page, paid upgrades
- **AI:** Yes — AI obituary generator
- **Revenue model:** Donations, flower partnerships, premium features
- **Weakness:** D2C model — no funeral home distribution channel. Generic templates. No newspaper research.

### ForeverMissed (forevermissed.com)
- **What:** Online memorial websites
- **Pricing:** Free basic, $35-99 for premium
- **Features:** Photos, videos, music, candle lighting, guest book
- **Weakness:** Dated design. No AI. No historical research. No B2B channel.

### Legacy.com
- **What:** The biggest obituary site in the US
- **Pricing:** Free to view, charges for posting obituaries
- **Revenue:** Advertising, partnerships with newspapers
- **Weakness:** Ad-heavy. Obituary-focused, not memorial-focused. No interactive/living features. Corporate feel.

### Echovita (echovita.com)
- **What:** Obituary aggregator
- **Traffic:** ~1.9M monthly visits (biggest competitor to EverLoved)
- **Weakness:** Aggregation, not creation. No family interaction features.

### Obituare (obituare.com)
- **What:** AI-powered obituary generator + memorial pages
- **Pricing:** Appears to be per-obituary
- **Features:** AI-generated obituaries, photo galleries, memory sharing
- **Weakness:** D2C only. New/small. No newspaper research. No B2B.

### Kudoboard
- **What:** Group tribute boards (not funeral-specific)
- **Weakness:** Generic — used for birthdays, retirements, etc. Not deep enough for memorials.

**Key insight:** The D2C players are either free (ad-supported) or cheap ($35-99). They compete on price and features. **None have a B2B funeral home distribution strategy. None do historical newspaper research.**

## Category 3: QR Code Memorial Products

This is the physical product side — QR codes on headstones that link to digital memorials.

### Our Tributes (ourtributes.com)
- **What:** QR memorial plaques for headstones + memorial webpages
- **Pricing:**
  - Single: $79.99 (plaque + webpage)
  - 5-19 units: $69.99 each (13% savings)
  - 20-50 units: $59.99 each (25% savings)
  - 50+: Custom pricing (up to 35% savings)
- **Features:** 2x2" Grade 316 stainless steel plaque, UV protective coating
- **Press:** Featured in NY Times 2024 Holiday Gift Guide
- **Selling on:** Amazon, own site
- **Weakness:** Basic memorial pages. No AI. No newspaper research. Product-focused, not platform-focused. Wholesale pricing suggests they're trying to sell to funeral homes but the model is per-plaque, not subscription.

### Life's QR (lifesqr.com)
- **What:** QR memorial plaques for headstones, urns, gardens
- **Pricing:** ~$100+ per product (TikTok promo code for $100 off suggests $200+ retail)
- **Features:** Multiple product lines, privacy options, custom-built website (ASP.NET)
- **Claims:** "Only industry recognized & trusted QR Memorial company"
- **Marketing:** TikTok influencer partnerships
- **Weakness:** Expensive. Hardware-focused. Memorial pages are basic.

### Living Headstones (monuments.com)
- **What:** QR codes etched into stone, linked to memorial profiles
- **Pricing:** $125 with headstone purchase (lifetime subscription)
- **Weakness:** Tied to headstone purchase from specific vendor. Not standalone.

### MemorialPics (photosforheadstones.com)
- **What:** Ceramic memorial photos with QR codes for headstones
- **Pricing:** Starting at $129.99
- **Weakness:** Photo-on-headstone product with QR as add-on. Not a platform.

### Monumark
- **What:** Memorial QR code services
- **Limited info available**

**Key insight:** QR memorial is a growing niche. Products range $60-200. Most are hardware-first companies that built basic memorial pages as an afterthought. **Nobody is combining premium AI-powered memorial pages with QR hardware in a B2B model.**

## Category 4: AI in Funeral Services

AI is entering the space but mostly for obituary writing, not full memorial creation.

### Gather AI Obituary Builder
- Generates obituary text from basic info input
- Staff or family inputs details, clicks "Generate"
- **No historical research, no newspaper discovery**

### EverLoved AI Obituary Writer
- Free AI-generated obituaries
- Template-based
- **No historical research**

### The Atlantic Article (June 2025)
- "A Computer Wrote My Mother's Obituary" — growing acceptance of AI in death care
- Funeral directors are already using AI for obituaries
- Families are increasingly okay with it
- **Signal:** The market is ready for AI-enhanced memorial products

**Key insight:** AI obituary writing is becoming normalized. But NOBODY is using AI to research the deceased's life through historical archives. **This is a wide-open lane.**

## The Moat

1. **Historical newspaper research** — nobody else does this. Finding a 1980 article about someone's grandfather building a house from chicken incubators is the kind of discovery that makes families cry. That's unforgettable and unreplicable by basic template sites.

2. **AI-powered narrative generation** — not just an obituary generator. A full life story woven from family input + historical research.

3. **B2B distribution** — funeral homes are the natural point of sale. They're already having the "how do you want to remember them?" conversation. This slots right in.

4. **Living memorial concept** — the page grows over time. Family members keep adding. This creates ongoing engagement and justifies annual hosting fees.

5. **Affordable standalone product** — funeral homes don't need to buy a $400/month full platform just to offer memorial pages.

## Risks & Considerations

- **Legal:** Using newspaper archives commercially — need to verify fair use / licensing for UFDC and other archives
- **Scalability:** Newspaper research currently requires scraping/searching — need to automate
- **Cemetery rules:** Some cemeteries don't allow QR codes or attachments on headstones
- **Competition response:** Gather or Tribute Tech could add newspaper research as a feature (but they're focused on bigger things)
- **Sales cycle:** Funeral homes are traditional/conservative — may be slow to adopt
- **Sensitivity:** Death care requires extreme care in marketing and tone
